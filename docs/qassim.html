      How it works

      1. Fields are mapped from ESA Sentinel-2 greenness: seasonal maximum NDVI from offset-corrected surface reflectance, 20 m.
      2. NISAR L-band radar (NASA-ISRO) gives three independent signals per field every 12 days: HV backscatter (crop canopy), 12-day coherence (surface being worked or irrigated) and soil moisture.
      3. A field is farmed in a season when its peak HV backscatter, relative to the same date's bare-desert median, beats a desert-patch tail threshold: median + 3 × (median − 16th percentile) of field-sized desert patches' seasonal peaks. The rule uses no Sentinel-2 input; Sentinel-2 is only the check.
      4. Water is a v1 benchmark estimate, not a radar measurement: farmed area × published irrigation depth by class ÷ pivot efficiency; energy = water × pumping head ÷ pump efficiency; CO₂ for grid or diesel pumps.

      How this differs from the v1 national run

      The v1 national run maps this ground too, in a separate run with fewer radar dates and a coarser grid, so the two are never added together.
